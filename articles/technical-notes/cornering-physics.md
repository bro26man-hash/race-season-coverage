# Cornering Physics — Technical Reference Notes

> **Source:** Wikipedia — "G-force" (key facts extracted for racing relevance)
> **Last updated:** Race season coverage setup

## 1. What Is G-Force?

- **G-force (gravitational force equivalent)** is a **mass-specific force** — force per unit mass — expressed in units of standard gravity (symbol **g** or **g₀**, not to be confused with grams).
- It is used for **sustained accelerations** that cause a perception of weight beyond simple gravity.
- An object at rest on Earth's surface experiences **1 g**, equaling gravitational acceleration of approximately **9.8 m/s²**.

## 2. Lateral G-Loads Through Corners

- When a car turns, the **lateral (side-to-side) G-load** is the horizontal acceleration felt by the driver and car.
- The g-force experienced is the **vector sum of all gravitational and non-gravitational forces** acting on the object's freedom to move.
- In corners, lateral G-forces are **transmitted through the car's structure and seat** into the driver's body via **surface-contact forces**.
- These forces create **interior mechanical stresses** throughout the car and driver — the chassis flexes, the seat pushes on the driver's sides, and the harness restrains the driver.

## 3. Centripetal Acceleration

- A car through a corner is undergoing **centripetal acceleration** — directed toward the center of the turn's radius.
- The required centripetal force increases with the **square of speed**: double the speed → four times the G-load.
- Drivers must **counter-steer** and modulate throttle/brake to maintain the precise centripetal force needed for the corner's radius without exceeding tire grip limits.
- The sensation of being "pushed to the outside" of a turn is the driver's body **resisting the centripetal acceleration** (inertia) — the seat and harness must supply the realcentripetal force.

## 4. The Role of Downforce

- **Downforce** is the aerodynamic down-push generated by wings, diffusers, and floor venturi tunnels.
- Downforce effectively **increases the normal force** between tires and track surface, which raises the **maximum friction force** available for cornering.
- Higher downforce = higher sustainable lateral G-loads before tire slip occurs.
- At high-speed circuits (e.g., Monza, Spa), teams run **minimal downforce** for straight-line speed; at twisty circuits (e.g., Monaco, Singapore), **maximum downforce** is prioritized for extreme cornering G-preview.
- Downforce scales with **dynamic pressure** (½ρv²), so it grows dramatically with speed — the car "sits into" the corners harder as velocity increases.

## 5. Physical Demands on Drivers

- Sustained lateral G-loads of **4–6 g** are common in high-speed corners; occasional spikes can reach **8–9 g** in extreme cases.
- The driver's **neck muscles** must resist the head's inertial moment — at 5 g lateral, a 5 kg helmet effectively weighs ~25 kg and creates a large yawing torque on the cervical spine.
- **Core and upper-body musculature** must brace against the lateral pushes from the seat and harness to maintain posture and precise control inputs.
- **Breathing is affected**: sustained high-G loading compresses the torso and makes it harder to take deep breaths, leading to elevated heart rates and accelerated fatigue.
- **Peripheral vision narrows** under high lateral G due to blood being pushed outward (g-LOC risk in extreme cases).
- Drivers undergo **specific neck-strengthening and G-tolerance training** to withstand these forces across a full race distance.

## 6. Practical Implications for Race Coverage

- When describing a corner, reference the **approximate lateral G-level** (e.g., "Turn 3 pulls 5.2 g") to give readers a visceral sense.
- Compare **downforce setups** between circuits to explain why grip and G-profiles differ.
- Note **driver body language** — head position, arm tension, breathing rate — as visible indicators of G-load severity.
- Use the **speed² relationship** to explain why braking points and turn-in speeds are so critical: a few km/h too fast at entry can push lateral Gs beyond the tire's envelope.
